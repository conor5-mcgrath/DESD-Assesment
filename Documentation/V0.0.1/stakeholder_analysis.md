Bristol Regional Food Network – Digital Marketplace
Stakeholder Analysis
1. Project Overview

The Bristol Regional Food Network Digital Marketplace is a platform designed to connect customers with local food producers. It will allow customers to search for products, add products from multiple producers to a basket, complete checkout and payment, and receive order and delivery or collection confirmations.

The project will focus on four modules:

Product exploration and searching
Basket management
Order confirmation
Delivery confirmation

The marketplace will calculate the network's 5% commission on successful orders. Payment functionality will use a sandbox or simulated payment service rather than a live payment system.

2. Key Stakeholders
Stakeholder	Role and Responsibilities	Main Interests
Project Supervisor	Provides academic guidance, reviews progress and deliverables, and ensures the project meets assessment requirements.	Quality, project scope, evidence of progress, and assessment criteria.
Project Manager and Developer	Coordinates the project, manages tasks and deadlines, communicates with the supervisor, and contributes to software development and testing.	Successful delivery, team coordination, code quality, and meeting deadlines.
Developers (3 team members)	Design, implement, integrate, and test the marketplace modules collaboratively.	Clear requirements, manageable workloads, functional software, and maintainable code.
Customers	Browse and search for products, manage baskets, purchase products from one or more producers, and receive confirmations.	Ease of use, accurate stock information, transparent pricing, secure test payments, and clear delivery or collection arrangements.
Food Producers	Provide product information, update stock and availability, receive order notifications, and prepare customer orders.	Accurate orders, inventory visibility, adequate preparation time, and clear order responsibilities.
Payment Service	Simulates payment authorisation and returns successful, declined, or temporarily unavailable responses.	Correct payment requests, reliable responses, and safe test transactions.
3. Stakeholder Needs and Priorities

Customers: The system must make it easy to find products, add items from different producers to a basket, review the total cost, and understand individual delivery or collection arrangements. Customers must be informed if stock is insufficient or payment fails.

Food producers: Producers need accurate order details, product availability information, and notifications of confirmed orders. The system should preserve the relationship between each order item and its producer.

Project supervisor: The supervisor needs evidence that the team is following a structured development process and meeting the project's objectives and assessment requirements.

Project manager and developers: The team needs clear requirements, agreed module responsibilities, a prioritised backlog, regular communication, and an integrated testing strategy.

Payment service: The payment integration must support successful payments, declined payments, and temporary service failures. All transactions must use a sandbox or simulated environment.

4. Team Structure and Responsibilities

The project will use the same five-person team throughout development.

Team Member/Role	Main Responsibilities
Project Supervisor	Academic oversight, feedback, and progress reviews.
Project Manager and Developer	Sprint planning, task coordination, progress monitoring, development, integration, and testing.
Developer 2	Product exploration and searching, including product listings and search functionality.
Developer 3	Basket management, including quantities, stock checks, and order totals.
Developer 4	Order confirmation, simulated payment integration, and delivery confirmation, working collaboratively with the other developers.

The module allocations are initial suggestions rather than strict boundaries. All developers will collaborate on integration, code reviews, testing, documentation, and resolving issues. The project manager will also contribute to implementation, and work will be reassigned where necessary to balance workloads.

Customers	Requirements discussions, feedback, and usability testing where available	During requirements gathering and testing

6. Key Stakeholder Risks
Unclear requirements: Confirm the expected behaviour of each module before implementation.
Uneven workloads: Review progress regularly and redistribute tasks when required.
Stock inaccuracies: Validate product availability and quantities during checkout.
Payment failures: Implement clear retry and exception-handling processes.
Integration problems: Agree on shared data structures and interfaces early, then test module integration regularly.
Limited producer technical experience: Keep producer-facing workflows simple and provide clear order information.
Insufficient preparation time: Where scheduling is supported, ensure order arrangements respect the required minimum 48-hour preparation lead time.
7. Conclusion

The stakeholder analysis identifies the main people and systems that influence the marketplace project. Customers and producers are the primary operational stakeholders, while the project supervisor provides academic oversight and the project manager coordinates delivery while contributing to development.

The five-person team will work collaboratively throughout the project to deliver the four agreed modules, with particular attention to stock validation, multi-producer orders, simulated payments, and clear customer confirmations.
